# Wallace D. Wattles — *The Science of Getting Rich* (1910)

Status: **Core canon.** Predates Hill by two decades and is a direct,
heavy influence on him — several of Hill's structures (definiteness of
purpose, acting "in a certain way") trace back to Wattles.

## Core mechanism

- **Acting in a "certain way"** — Wattles's central claim is that getting
  rich is not about *where* you act (the right job, the right market) but
  *how* you act: efficiently, purposefully, and in a way that consistently
  creates more value than you consume, done in every transaction, no
  exceptions. This is the through-line the agent should extract: **consistent,
  growth-oriented action beats passive wishing**, and Wattles explicitly
  argues against the version of "manifestation" that skips action.
- Wattles frames desire/thought as the *starting* step, not the whole
  process — the book's own structure moves from "form a clear mental image"
  to "then you must act, today, in your present business/job, in the certain
  way" every single chapter. The pop-culture flattening of Wattles into pure
  visualization skips this.
- **Gratitude as a discipline**, in Wattles's system, functions less as a
  metaphysical lever and more as an attention-management tool: it keeps
  focus on what's working and reinforces the behaviors that produced it —
  translatable to modern habit-reinforcement framing.

## What's actually defensible

- The "certain way" instruction — do your current work more efficiently and
  purposefully than is required, every time — is a genuine, well-supported
  performance principle (excellence and reputation compounding), independent
  of any metaphysical framing.
- The insistence that thought alone is insufficient without action is worth
  preserving explicitly; it's one of the few points in this canon where the
  original author pre-empts the "just visualize it" misreading that later
  popularizers (see `modern-claims.md`) leaned into.

## What to flag if surfaced at face value

- Wattles's claim that thought operates on a literal "formless substance"
  that shapes matter is metaphysical assertion, not mechanism — never
  present as validated.
