# Florence Scovel Shinn — *The Game of Life and How to Play It* (1925)

Status: **Core canon, practical/affirmation-based variant.** Shinn is the
most explicitly religious/affirmation-driven of the core canon (heavy use
of scripture and short spoken affirmations as the primary technique), and
the most direct ancestor of the modern affirmation-card/vision-board
genre.

## Core content

- **Affirmation as the primary practice.** Short, repeated, present-tense
  statements ("I now attract...") used before/during action, framed by
  Shinn as spiritual law. The defensible mechanism underneath: brief
  self-affirmation exercises have real, measured effects on stress
  reduction, confidence, and performance under pressure in psychology
  research (self-affirmation theory) — the agent should use *this* framing,
  not Shinn's literal "spoken word as law" claim.
- Shinn frequently pairs affirmation with a required concrete action ("speak
  the word, then take the indicated step") — closer in structure to Wattles
  than the pure-affirmation reading of her work suggests.
- Introduces the "game" framing — approaching goal pursuit with equanimity
  and detachment from anxious outcome-fixation, which overlaps with
  legitimate performance-psychology advice about reducing outcome-anxiety to
  improve execution.

## What's actually defensible

- Brief self-affirmation as a pre-performance stress/confidence tool is
  supported by self-affirmation theory research — frame it that way, as a
  short priming practice, not a spiritual law.
- Reducing anxious outcome-fixation to improve execution quality is
  consistent with sports/performance psychology.

## What to flag if surfaced at face value

- Shinn's claims are the most explicitly religious/metaphysical in the core
  canon (affirmations as literal spiritual law, direct divine
  intervention in circumstances). Present as historical/devotional context
  only, never as validated mechanism.
