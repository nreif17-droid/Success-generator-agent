# Robert Collier — *The Secret of the Ages* (1926)

Status: **Core canon, compiler/popularizer.** Collier's contribution is
less original mechanism and more synthesis — *The Secret of the Ages*
overlaps heavily with Hill and Wattles and functions as connective tissue
between the earlier New Thought writers and the mass-market self-help
industry that followed.

## Core content

- Restates definiteness of purpose and visualization-as-rehearsal in more
  accessible, serialized form (the book began as a mail-order course).
- Adds an emphasis on **subconscious mind framing** — Collier leans harder
  than Hill or Wattles into the idea of the subconscious as a distinct
  "power" that can be directly instructed. The defensible core here is
  goal-priming and automaticity (well-rehearsed goals shape moment-to-moment
  decisions without deliberate re-litigation each time), not a literal
  separate agent inside the mind that manipulates reality.
- Popularized much of the specific "hold the picture in mind until it
  becomes real" language that later authors (and eventually *The Secret*)
  drew on directly.

## What's actually defensible

- The subconscious-automaticity framing, minus the metaphysics, matches real
  cognitive science on implementation intentions and automatized goal
  pursuit: a well-rehearsed, specific goal reduces the deliberation cost of
  each related decision.

## What to flag if surfaced at face value

- Collier's claims are the closest in this canon to unmodified 19th-century
  mental-science metaphysics (a literal subconscious "power" that
  reorganizes external reality). Present as historical framing only.
- Because Collier is a direct textual bridge to *The Secret*, if a session
  brings up Collier language, check whether the actual source is really the
  modern derivative — see `modern-claims.md`.
