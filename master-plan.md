# Cross-Venture Master Plan

Built 2026-08-05, in response to a direct request for an in-depth,
honest depiction of how all five ventures fit together over the next 12
months — where they overlap, where they don't, and what that actually
means for Nolan's own time and attention, not just an abstract org chart.

This file sits above the individual `domains/*.md` files — it's the
integration layer, not a replacement for any of them. Refresh it whenever
a venture's status materially changes (a new session, a missed/hit
milestone), not on a fixed schedule.

## 3. Capacity reality check — the actual constraint

This is the part worth being blunt about, because `sessions/CURRENT-
STATE.md` already flagged it as a pattern showing up in every venture
built so far, not a one-off concern.

- **Agency:** 10–15 hr/week ongoing baseline — known, committed.
- **Sales job:** structurally at least ~9–10 hours on a working day (1hr
  drive each way + 8am start + appointments running to 6pm or later per
  the example day on file) — but **the actual number of working
  days/week has not been confirmed.** The appointment worksheet lists
  Mon–Sat as possible days, which — if it's really up to 6 days/week —
  would put sales job alone at 54–66+ hours/week. This is the single
  biggest unknown in this entire plan and it matters more than anything
  else here: **confirm actual scheduled days/week once the job starts,
  and revisit every number below once that's real instead of assumed.**
- **Apparel:** currently low (playbook-building, vendor outreach) — will
  rise once execution starts, but execution is gated on capital that
  doesn't exist yet, so this stays low through most of this timeline by
  default, not by discipline.
- **Grid:** "daily/weekly attention" mentioned in `domains/grid-
  startup.md` but no specific hours defined — currently just outreach for
  research purposes.
- **Coaching:** 0.
- **Relational/family time, rest, the counter-move practice itself:**
  not hour-quantified anywhere on file, but named as a top vision
  priority (`vision.md` — relational goals) and can't just be whatever's
  left over after everything else.

**The honest read:** if sales job turns out to be close to full-time
(5–6 days/week at the pace described), there is very little slack left
for agency's already-committed 10–15 hr/week, let alone apparel, grid, or
the relational time named as a top-tier vision priority. This isn't a
reason not to run all of this — it's a reason to **sequence deliberately
rather than assume everything runs at full intensity simultaneously
starting 2026-08-10.**

## 4. Overlap vs. non-overlap between ventures

- **Agency ↔ Grid: real overlap, not competition.** Same underlying
  skill being built — AI-driven automation, outreach, selling a service,
  learning how both Claude and a business actually operate. Grid is
  explicitly the escalation of the agency's model, not a separate skill
  set. Time spent proving out the agency **is** time spent building
  toward grid.
- **Agency ↔ Apparel: capital-linked, operationally separate.** Agency
  is outreach/service work; apparel is vendor/product management. They
  don't share a skill thread the way agency/grid do — they compete for
  time and attention, not for the same kind of work.
- **Sales job ↔ everything else: no overlap, pure capacity competition.**
  In-person appointment-based sales is a genuinely different skill and
  industry from the AI-agency/apparel/grid cluster. Its only relationship
  to the rest of the portfolio is how many hours it leaves available for
  the others.
- **Coaching: currently isolated** — dormant, no interaction with
  anything else on this timeline.

## 6. Personal relationship with this portfolio — the honest diagnosis

Pulling together what's already been named across the individual
sessions, not inventing a new read:

- **The self-assessed weak points on file** (`vision.md`): lack of
  clarity/systems, spending too much time not doing enough, not holding
  himself to a high enough standard. The risk this year isn't lack of
  ambition — there's clearly enough of that to run five ventures at
  once. **The risk is running all of them at once without a forcing
  function to sequence them**, which is exactly the pattern this
  document exists to counter.
- **Each venture built so far has independently flagged the same
  underlying tension** (confirmed 3-for-3 in `sessions/CURRENT-STATE.md`):
  time/capacity conflict with at least one other venture. That's not
  three separate problems — it's one structural problem showing up three
  times.
- **The apparel session named the sharpest version of this directly:**
  the real risk isn't avoiding apparel — apparel's creative work is more
  comfortable than cold outreach or sales appointments, so the actual
  risk is using it as an escape from the harder, higher-priority work.
  That risk doesn't go away by building a good system for apparel — it
  gets *easier* to rationalize once apparel has a polished, real-feeling
  operation behind it. Worth naming again here, not just once in that
  session.
- **What the priority order already on file is actually for:** agency
  first isn't arbitrary — it's the funding source for apparel and the
  proof-of-concept for grid. Protecting agency's 10–15 hr/week baseline
  is structurally more important than it looks day-to-day, because
  under-investing there doesn't just slow the agency — it delays capital
  for apparel and stalls the track record grid depends on.
- **Sales job is the wildcard**, not because it's a bad decision, but
  because its real time cost isn't confirmed yet, and everything above
  assumes agency's baseline holds regardless. If sales job turns out to
  be closer to full-time than expected, the honest move is dropping
  apparel's time investment further (it's already the most
  capital-gated, lowest-urgency piece) — not agency's.
