## Fulfillment model: small-batch bulk

Decided 2026-08-05: **small-batch bulk** (manufacturer with low minimums,
roughly 50–150 units per design), not print-on-demand/dropship and not a
large warehouse order. Rationale: POD/dropship trades away product
quality control and brand culture — both explicitly named as priorities
("legitimate long-term business," "a product I care about," "build a
culture around it"). Small-batch bulk keeps that control while bounding
capital exposure to what a low-MOQ run costs, scaling batch size up as
capital and demand actually prove out.

## Business structure & financing (informational, not legal/financial advice)

- **Entity:** apparel needs its own LLC, separate from the agency's —
  different risk profile (physical consumer product / liability exposure
  vs. a service business), and separate entities keep profitability
  tracking clean per venture. Confirm specifics with an actual
  accountant/attorney before filing.
- **Financing:** a new, pre-revenue LLC won't qualify for a traditional
  bank/SBA loan on its own — those require revenue history the venture
  doesn't have yet. Realistic early options (personal loan, business
  credit card, SBA microloan, purchase-order financing tied to a
  confirmed manufacturer order) almost all require a **personal
  guarantee** — meaning the LLC's liability shield doesn't cover that
  specific debt. Treat any of these as a real personal financial decision,
  not a shortcut around the agency-capital timeline, especially while
  income is about to shift to commission-based sales.

## 1. Market research

- **Ongoing cadence, not a one-time step:** monthly check-in (tie to the
  monthly point of whichever weekly review cadence ends up covering this
  venture) — competitor pricing/positioning scan, one round of direct
  audience feedback on current designs (even informal — DMs, a poll),
  and a trend-relevance check (is the design direction still resonating,
  or has it drifted).
- **Before the first bulk order specifically:** validate at least one
  design with real audience reaction (posted content, direct feedback, or
  pre-order interest) before committing capital to it — don't let the
  first real money spent be on an unvalidated design.

## 2. Vendor / supplier management

- **Selection criteria (documented once, reused every time):** minimum
  order quantity fits small-batch range (50–150 units), sample turnaround
  time, quoted production lead time, quality of a paid sample (not just
  photos), and at least one verifiable reference or review history.
- **Process:** request samples from 2–3 candidate manufacturers before
  committing → evaluate against the criteria above → select primary
  vendor → identify one backup vendor (not used yet, just identified) so
  a primary-vendor failure isn't a from-scratch search.
- **Reorder trigger:** define a specific inventory threshold (e.g., "when
  a design drops below 15 units on hand") that triggers a reorder
  conversation — decided for real once first inventory exists, not
  invented here from zero data.
- **Communication cadence with active vendor:** a check-in point at each
  production milestone (order placed → in production → shipped) rather
  than ad hoc follow-up.

## 3. Product & inventory

- **SKU structure:** each design × each size = one SKU, tracked
  individually (not just "how many shirts total") so reorder decisions
  are per-design, not aggregate.
- **Inventory tracking:** a simple running count per SKU (units received
  − units sold = units on hand), reviewed at the same cadence as the
  weekly review once real inventory exists.
- **Quality control:** spot-check a sample of each bulk shipment on
  arrival against the approved sample before it's treated as sellable
  inventory.

## 4. Order fulfillment (once live)

- **Process, order to ship:** order received (via whichever sales channel
  is finalized) → pulled from inventory → packed → shipped → tracking
  sent to customer. Target turnaround time to be set once real order
  volume exists to set it against (don't commit to an SLA with zero data
  on how long packing/shipping actually takes at this scale).
- **Returns/exchanges:** a written policy decided *before* the first sale,
  not improvised on the first return request.
- **Shipping carrier:** compare rates once real package dimensions/weights
  exist from the actual product — premature to lock in now.

## 5. Profitability tracking

- **Per-unit economics to track, once real numbers exist:** cost per unit
  (manufacturing + shipping-to-you), sale price, payment-processing fee,
  shipping-to-customer cost, resulting margin per unit. This is a
  framework to fill in with real numbers, not a promised margin — actual
  profitability depends on real vendor quotes and real sale price, not
  projected here.
- **Review cadence:** monthly profitability review once selling starts —
  margin per SKU, which designs are actually profitable vs. not, and one
  adjustment decision per review (price, vendor, or design lineup).
- **Break-even framing:** track how many units per design need to sell to
  cover that design's bulk order cost — a concrete number once a real
  vendor quote exists, not estimated here from nothing.

## 6. Scaling / automation roadmap

Automation triggers, defined now so the decision is pre-made rather than
made under pressure later — thresholds are placeholders to be sanity
-checked against real numbers once the venture has actual volume:

- **Order volume threshold for order-management software:** once manual
  order tracking (spreadsheet-level) becomes the bottleneck — likely
  somewhere in the tens of orders/week range, to be confirmed against
  real experience, not assumed now.
- **Inventory volume threshold for 3PL (third-party fulfillment):** once
  in-house packing/shipping time meaningfully competes with the other
  ventures' time demands (agency, sales job) — this is explicitly a
  capacity trigger, not just a revenue one, given how tightly time is
  already allocated across ventures.
- **Marketing automation:** once organic/manual content posting has a
  track record of what converts, automate the *distribution* of proven
  content — not before there's a proven pattern to automate.
- **Principle across all three:** automate what's already been proven to
  work manually, not what's guessed to work — matches the same "prove it
  by hand first" discipline used in the agency's outreach system.
